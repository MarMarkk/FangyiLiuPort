01

Project overview

APlus is an academic app concept for wearable health technology, exploring how devices such as Apple Watch, Oura Ring and Whoop could connect to a more understandable and supportive health experience. Our three-person team researched why people disengage from wearables and explored ways to reduce confusion, pressure and notification overload.

02

My contribution

  • Heavy User research: I conducted the Heavy User interviews and analyzed this group’s behaviors, needs and pain points.
  • Prototype & final design: I independently designed and built the app prototype and made the final design solution decisions.
  • Testing & iteration: I shared the prototype with users across all three groups for testing. After reviewing the feedback and identifying issues with my team, I independently completed the design revisions.

Team collaboration: We discussed and developed the broader problem framing, research synthesis, ideation and evaluation of user feedback together.

03

Three user groups, one shared challenge

Our team conducted 9 semi-structured interviews across three user groups. I focused on Heavy Users; we brought our findings together to understand the wider experience.

  • Heavy Users: Rely on health data but can feel pressure or anxiety. They need supportive feedback and sustainable engagement.
  • Paused Users: Have stepped back because of stress, fatigue or low motivation. They need clearer value and a less overwhelming experience.
  • Niche Users: Use devices selectively and may have limited trust. They need understandable data and personalized tracking.

Our shared insight: engagement depends on trust and emotional experience, not just the number of features.

04

The design solution

Building on our team discussions, I decided on the final design solution and independently translated it into an interactive app prototype.

  • Clearer health insights: Plain-language explanations and an AI-supported insight concept help users interpret complex metrics.
  • Supportive daily guidance: Practical next steps replace pressure-driven feedback and achievement messaging.
  • More control: Rest Mode and tracking suggestions explore a calmer, more adaptable wearable experience.

The prototype connects onboarding and wearable setup to a daily dashboard, health insights and recovery guidance.

05

User testing & my revisions

The prototype was tested with 6 participants across our Heavy, Paused and Niche User groups. Users explored it independently, without an introduction, before answering evaluation questions. My team and I reviewed their feedback together and identified the key issues; I then independently revised the prototype.

  • “What should I do next?” I turned insight cards into a daily action plan with concrete, manageable steps.
  • Metrics needed explanation. I added plain-language context and reduced raw data on the main screens.
  • Goals could feel judgmental. I reduced achievement pressure and used more supportive messaging.
  • Rest Mode pop-ups felt intrusive. I removed the pop-up while retaining the recovery feature.
06

What I learned

Turning research into a prototype and revising it myself after testing showed me how easily helpful health information can become overwhelming. Clearer explanations, actionable guidance and greater user control became central to my design approach.

Our discussions of ethical concerns also taught me to question what information belongs in a health app. Calorie counts and prompts to photograph or log meals may add anxiety or encourage excessive monitoring for some users. I learned to consider these emotional risks before including a feature, rather than assuming more tracking is always helpful.

For me, responsible health app design means protecting sensitive data, avoiding judgmental language and giving users a meaningful choice about what they track and share. Supporting well-being includes knowing when to leave information out.
